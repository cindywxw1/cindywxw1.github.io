I like problems where the answer has to survive contact with real hardware. Most of what I build starts on a bench: a scope probe on a test point, a logic analyzer catching an edge that arrives 30 milliseconds too late, a board I laid out myself that needs one more rework wire before it behaves.

At the Manufacturing Futures Institute I build sensor nodes that listen to industrial machines and tell you when they are about to fail. In my own time I am writing a pipelined limit order book in SystemVerilog, which has turned into a long argument with the timing report. I also teach: this fall I take over as head TA for 15-122, running a team of more than forty TAs.

Outside of coursework I am on Carnegie Mellon Racing's perception hardware, and I spend Build18 week making things that have no business existing, like a record player built from scratch.

High-frequency order book feed handler

Jun 2026 — present

A six-stage pipelined limit order book in SystemVerilog, targeting 250 MHz with one message retired per cycle. Book storage lives in BRAM with 16 price levels per side; because the read is synchronous I could not scan for the best bid and ask combinationally, so the top of book is tracked in its own pipeline stage to close timing.

SystemVerilog · Vivado · VCS

FPGA digital system design and verification

Spring 2026

Hierarchical SystemVerilog circuits — a magic square validator and a character decoder — built by unit-testing each submodule before integration. I minimized the combinational logic with K-maps, then compared NAND against NOR implementations by reading LUT utilization out of Vivado synthesis, and deployed the result to a Boolean Board for hardware verification.

SystemVerilog · Vivado · Boolean Board

images/lidar-sync.png

LiDAR and camera hardware triggering

Jul 2025 — May 2026

Cone detection on the racing team was suffering because the LiDAR and the cameras saw the world at different moments. I bench-tested clock-sync and semi-sync strategies on a logic analyzer, then moved the team to a direct hardware trigger from an STM32, which cut the gap from over 30 ms to under one.

STM32 · C · Logic analyzer · ROS2

Analog vinyl record player with DJ set

Build18, Jan 2026

A record player built from the platter up in a week. The signal chain is a custom pre-amplifier at 100× gain with live bass and treble control; the pre-amp and power distribution boards are my layouts, hand-soldered SMD. PID firmware holds the platter at speed, and the enclosure is laser-cut acrylic on mounting rings modeled in SolidWorks.

Altium · SMD soldering · PID control · SolidWorks
